Introduction

Child malnutrition remains an important public health challenge in Nigeria. It can take different forms, including stunting, wasting and underweight, which reflect different aspects of nutritional and growth problems in children under five.

Why does this matter?

Nigeria has one of the highest burdens of child malnutrition in sub-Saharan Africa. Understanding where malnutrition is concentrated, and what factors are associated with it, is a necessary first step toward effective intervention. This project examines the distribution of three malnutrition indicators among children under five using data from the 2024 Nigeria Demographic and Health Survey.

Understanding the Indicators

Stunting

Stunting means a child's height is too low for their age. It reflects chronic, long-term nutritional deprivation and is measured using the height-for-age Z-score. A child is classified as stunted if their Z-score falls more than two standard deviations below the WHO median.

Wasting

Wasting means a child's weight is too low for their height. It reflects acute or severe food shortage and can develop rapidly. Wasting is associated with increased risk of death in young children.

Underweight

Underweight means a child's weight is too low for their age. It is a general indicator of overall nutritional status and can reflect elements of both stunting and wasting.
